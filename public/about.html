What this is

Every day you place five political ideas on a grid. Left to right is economics, and bottom to top runs from libertarian to authoritarian. After each one you see where it sits and how close you were.

Where the positions come from

Nobody can say exactly where an idea belongs, so Compassle doesn't guess. Each idea sits at the average position of the political parties that give it the most backing or attention, taken from two academic datasets.

  • Chapel Hill Expert Survey 2024 (CHES). Political scientists rate parties in 31 European countries on left-right, on a cultural scale, and on individual issues. For an idea such as free trade, Compassle finds the parties that most strongly back it and averages where they sit.
  • Manifesto Project Dataset 2026a. Researchers code how much of each election manifesto goes to each topic. For ideas such as military strength or trade unions, Compassle averages the positions of manifestos (since 2010, from 62 countries) that give the topic the most space.

What to keep in mind

  • Parties that back an idea are not the idea itself. A position here is a guide, not a verdict.
  • CHES covers Europe only, so many cards reflect European politics.
  • CHES's vertical scale (green/libertarian to traditional/authoritarian) is a close cousin of libertarian-authoritarian, not the same thing. The Manifesto Project's vertical axis is built by Compassle from cultural categories and is rougher.
  • Averages pull toward the middle, so many ideas sit closer to the centre than you might expect.

Privacy

Compassle has no accounts, no cookies and no advertising. Your scores, streaks and progress are stored in your own browser. Clearing site data removes them, so use Export in "Your record" if you want a copy.

When you finish a daily puzzle, the site sends one anonymous record to be counted: the puzzle number, your total and your score on each idea. It carries no name, ID, cookie or IP address, and nothing links it to you or to your other games. It's used only to see how many people play and which ideas are hard.
